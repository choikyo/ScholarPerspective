## Takeaways

- **Three tiers:** free ($0), small/regional ($50–$100), and big commercial publishers (~$1,600–$2,200; up to ~$3,500 for niche titles).
- **No-fee is the norm in the humanities.** Across DOAJ, roughly 63–73% of all listed journals charge no APC; humanities make up ~14% of DOAJ journals but only ~1% of fee-charging ones (figures as reported by studies in the search results below — not independently verified).
- **Every fee-charging commercial journal above offers a waiver** — consistent with DOAJ's expectations if fees are ever introduced.
- **None of these charge "other fees"** (submission, page, color) — fees are concentrated in a single APC charged on acceptance.
- **Fees grow with reputation:** SAGE Open went from free → $99 → $395 → $800 (after Web of Science indexing) → $1,500 (2023) → $1,600 today. A useful precedent for a "free now, revisit later" approach.

## For Contrast — Science & Medicine Journals

These are the "thousands of dollars" end of the market. Different economics from humanities: grant-funded research, where the author's funder or institution usually pays.

### Takeaways (science side)

- Science APCs run roughly **10–100× humanities APCs**: ~$2,200–$3,700 for large "mega-journals", ~$5,500–$7,500 for selective OA titles, up to ~$12,850 for *Nature*.
- Price tracks prestige and selectivity, not production cost.
- In hybrid journals (Nature, Cell, Lancet) authors can publish for free behind a paywall — the fee only buys open access.
- Many authors never pay personally: institutional "read and publish" deals cover the fee.
- Waivers are near-universal here too.
